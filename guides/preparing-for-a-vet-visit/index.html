THE HORSE OWNER'S FIELD GUIDE

Make every visit count.

STRETTON HILLS VETS · SHROPSHIRE

A little preparation makes veterinary appointments easier for horses, owners and clinicians. These are practical things to consider when arranging a routine equine visit.

Share the right details when booking

Tell the practice your horse's name, the reason for the appointment and where the horse will be located. If the concern is recent, explain when you first noticed it and whether anything has changed. Mention relevant treatments or medicines so the team can advise what information they need.

Think about access and facilities

Before the appointment, consider whether the vet will be able to find the yard and park safely. Depending on the planned examination, the practice may discuss having a suitable, well-lit area available and arranging someone familiar with the horse to be present. Ask the team what is appropriate for your particular visit rather than assuming every appointment is the same.

Keep useful information to hand

  • Your horse's passport or identification details, if requested.
  • Relevant treatment history or a list of medicines, where available.
  • Any questions you would like answered during the appointment.
  • A phone number on which you can be reached.

Ask about the next steps

At the end of a routine visit, make sure you understand any recommended follow-up, the timing of future checks, and how to contact the practice if circumstances change. It is reasonable to ask for an explanation of unfamiliar terms.

When a concern cannot wait

This guide is for planning non-urgent appointments only. If you believe your horse needs urgent veterinary attention, telephone the practice directly. Do not rely on email or a website message to request emergency help.
